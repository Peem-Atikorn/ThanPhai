Module: Hazard Risk Models
Process and techniques

This module produces risk evidence for four hazards. The LLM must not guess these results.

Scope rule
- This is risk nowcasting from official forecasts and observations, not weather simulation.
- Use TMD forecasts as inputs for rain and storm; do not build a new weather model.

Techniques per hazard
- Rain / storm: rules on forecast rain and wind first, then a classifier that adjusts for local history.
- Flood: gradient boosting on accumulated rain, water level trend, reservoir level, terrain, and past floods.
- Drought: SPI and NDVI or soil moisture anomalies combined into a drought index; trend for 1–3 months.
- Fire: classifier on dryness, weather, vegetation, and recent hotspots; strong seasonal patterns in the north (Feb–Apr).

Training and evaluation
- Split data by time (train on past seasons, test on later seasons) and by area to prevent leakage.
- Focus on recall and false negatives for WARNING and CRITICAL cases.
- Report precision, recall, F1, Brier score, and a calibration plot for each hazard.
- Compare every model with its rule-based baseline and keep the baseline if the model is not clearly better.
- Evaluate on real past events, for example the 2011 floods, recent northern fire seasons, and recent drought years.

Explainability and safety
- Use SHAP or rule hits as reason codes, and do not describe them as proof of cause.
- Rule-based safety overrides always apply, for example water above bank level means at least WARNING.
- Official warnings are passed to module 07 and are never lowered by the model.

Monitoring
- Track drift by area, season, and hazard.
- Track the false negative rate against ground reports and later observations from module 08.
